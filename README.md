# gravity-holder
A 3D printed film holder for DSLR scanning.


![the gravity holder screenshot from Fusion 360](/images/rear.png)
For more images see the `images` directory.

## What is it and Why I made it?
I wanted a scanning rig for my DSLR that would hold the film really **still and flat** so that when I am cropping in my editor, I can just apply the same crop to the whole roll.
I also wanted a holder that would be modular and have its own "bellows" to keep the ambient light out. This is the result of my work.

The way it works is you have a sliding part that pushes the top pressure plate up and allows you to slide the film roll without scratching it.
The bottom pressure plate is recessed just deep enough for the roll to not bow up and escape the groove.

When you slide the slider back, gravity presses the top pressure plate firmly against the film roll.
In my experience, this leaves the film really flat. Much flatter than my Epson scanner frame or the S-curve based 3D printed holders I tried.

> You might be concerned about the film roll sliding against the bottom pressure plate and it scratching the emulsion.

In my experience, that isn't something that happens. I have been using it with Fomapan, a film known to be kind of easy to damage and haven't had any issues. I think you can safely rely on the film's natural bow curve that occurs as the film dries. Because of it, the film is mostly in contact with the bottom pressure plate along the edges. The top pressure plate lifts high enough and leaves plenty of room for the glossy surface of the film to not get scratched either. In my experience, it is plenty safe and gentle on the film. You just have to make sure to always lift the top pressure plate first before you go pull on the film roll.

> I designed it to fit my `Canon EF 50mm f/2.5 Compact Macro` lens with an additional **13mm macro-tube** attached to it.

However, you can adjust the dimensions of the tube that fits the lens to your own lens of choice pretty easily.

I made it so that you can add some magnets, but you don't really have to use them. If you have at least modestly heavy camera body or lens (or both), gravity will do its job just fine. I use Canon 600D and together with the lens it is plenty heavy for it to work (and it's not even a full-frame body).

It is supposed to fit the CineStill CS-Lite-Brite - it goes on top of it where their original cutout masks go. But you can probably adapt it to almost any light with a bit of work.

It is all pretty modular. Pieces can be swapped and modified to fit tightly based on your own printer.


> I am already planning on designing a V2. I realized that it might have been better if the sliding mechanism was orthogonal to the film roll so that my fingers can avoid avoiding the film roll during sliding. Something that appeared obvious pretty much the first moment I had it assembled. The V2 is currently not in the works, it will take some time, I am working on other projects.


## How to Make it
Download the `.step` files from the `parts` directory and print them on your preferred setting. Alternatively, you can download the `Gravity Holder v1.3mf` file and load it into a compatible slicer.
It uses some standard M3 heat inserts and screws to hold everything together. You may use magnets but you don't have to.


## How to modify it
Download and import the Fusion 360 archive files from the `fusion` directory. You should be able to figure out the rest.
